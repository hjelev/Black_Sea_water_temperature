Why is the sea in Cape Kaliakra, Bulgaria sometimes suddenly cold?

Because of upwelling. When a steady wind blows along the coast or away from it for a day or two, it pushes the warm surface layer out to sea and cold water from below rises to replace it. The water can cool by 5–8°C within a day, even in midsummer, and usually warms up again a few days after the wind changes. Are there jellyfish in the sea at Cape Kaliakra, Bulgaria?

Sometimes. The most common is the moon jellyfish (Aurelia aurita), whose sting is very weak. In late summer the large barrel jellyfish (Rhizostoma pulmo) can appear; its sting causes mild, short-lived irritation. The transparent comb jellies don't sting at all. Numbers peak in warm water from July to September and vary a lot from year to year.

Are there tides in the Black Sea at Cape Kaliakra, Bulgaria?

Practically none. The Black Sea is almost enclosed, so the tidal range is only a few centimetres. Changes in the water level at Cape Kaliakra, Bulgaria come from wind and waves, not tides.
